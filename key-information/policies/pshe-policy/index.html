PSHE September 2025

Aims and priorities for subject teaching and learning

Our school mission statement reflects what we hope to achieve in PSHE and Citizenship and is underpinned by our whole school values - Respect, Kind and Curious.

‘We are a caring school community, where everyone is respected and valued. We can learn from each other in a safe and supportive environment, having the confidence to meet challenges with enthusiasm and a belief that anything is possible.’

The intent for our children is to develop self- awareness, positive self-esteem and confidence, enabling them to,

  • stay as healthy as possible
  • keep themselves and others safe
  • have worthwhile and fulfilling relationships
  • respect the differences between people
  • develop independence and responsibility
  • play an active role as members of a democratic society
  • make the most of their own abilities and those of others
  • behave in a socially and morally acceptable way including towards authority and each other
  • to become involved in the life of their community
  • to know about democracy and how to be an active citizens
  • to know about economic wellbeing.

We want our children to: -

  • value the achievements they make, and the achievements of others
  • make informed choices about dealing with risks and meeting challenges now and in the future.
  • Decide on values by which they want to live their lives.
  • Know strategies in order to have a good understanding of their mental health.
  • Feel confident to share their worries by using the ‘Ask it’ basket in KS1 and the ‘Worry Monster’ in EYFS.
  • Children to regulate their own emotions and have strategies to do this - ‘Zones of Regulation’ are used in all classrooms to help with this.

Legal Requirement

It is compulsory to teach Health Education and Relationships Education aspects of PSHE in primary schools (from September 2020).

Sources used for the scheme of work

The whole school use The Jigsaw Scheme which classes access online. This is regularly updated. In reception, Personal, Social and Emotional development (PSED) is a prime area. To embed the children's knowledge thoroughly we also plan activities and provisions weekly in order to achieve the Early Learning Goal (ELG) from the Early Years Foundation stage Framework.

Aim of the Jigsaw PSHE programme:

To provide pupils with the knowledge, understanding, attitudes, values and skills they need in order to reach their potential as individuals and within the community.

Pupils are encouraged to take part in a wide range of activities and experiences across and beyond the curriculum, contributing fully to the life of their school and communities. In doing so they learn the curriculum, contributing fully to the life of their school and communities. In doing so they learn to recognise their own worth, work well with others and become increasingly responsible for their own learning. They reflect on their experiences and understand how they are developing personally and socially, tackling many of the spiritual, moral, social and cultural issues that are part of growing up.

They learn to understand and respect our common humanity; diversity and differences so that they can go on to form the effective, fulfilling relationships that are an essential part of life and learning.

There are 6 puzzles in Jigsaw that are designed to progress in sequence from September -July. The progression and impact can clearly be seen in the Jigsaw Scheme of work, which can be found on the staff drive and also a copy is kept in each year group.

These are:

  • Autumn 1- Being me in my World
  • Autumn 2- Celebrating Difference (including anti-bullying)
  • Spring 1- Dreams and Goals
  • Spring 2- Healthy Me
  • Summer 1- Relationships
  • Summer 2- Changing Me (including sex education- see SRE policy)

Jigsaw brings together PSHE education, emotional literacy, social skills and spiritual development in a comprehensive scheme of learning. Jigsaw will support the development of the skills, attitudes, values and behaviour. It also helps children to develop self-awareness, motivation, social skills and managing their feelings. All classes have a worry monster or ‘Ask it’ basket to address any additional worries or issues the children may have.

These skills help our children to become better learners, get on better with other people and be responsible citizens. They are part of every area of life.

We use a range of strategies to provide a breadth of effective learning opportunities including active learning, Circle Time, co-operative games including parachute games, enquiry, discussion and participation in citizenship activities such as School Council elections. Children work together on projects, make decisions, take responsibility and reflect on their experiences.

Our ethos and PSHE curriculum ensure that we meet the 5 strands of Every Child Matters which states that every child has the right to: Be Healthy, Stay Safe, Enjoy and Achieve, Make a Positive Contribution, and Achieve Economic Well-being.

Visitors

Visitors are a valuable resource in supporting our PSHE and citizenship programme.

The following people are visitors to our school:

Local Clergy and Christian Youth Workers.

Representatives of charities which are being supported.

Other personnel and workers from our community are invited into school as appropriate. Visits are organised by Year Group Leaders where possible.

We have a mindfulness coach who works with the Y2 children once a week. She teaches them strategies for self regulation. This is reviewed annually depending on funding.

Strategies for teaching

Teaching strategies are varied and are mindful of preferred learning styles and the need for differentiation. Teachers establish safe, open and positive learning environments based on trusting relationships between all members of the class, adults and children alike. All teachers establish ‘ground rules’ that are agreed and owned at the beginning of the year.

All classes devise ‘ground rules’ which are drawn up with the class at the beginning of the year and are reinforced during every piece that is taught throughout the year. This is known as the class ‘Jigsaw Charter’ which is displayed in every class. Whilst the charters are tailored to individual classes, they include all of the aspects outlined in the Jigsaw scheme of work. These are:

  • We take turns to speak.
  • We use kind and positive words.
  • We listen to each other.
  • We have the right to pass.
  • We only use names when giving compliments or when being positive.
  • We respect our different views and opinions.
  • We respect each other’s privacy (confidentiality).
  • We know that if we say something about ourselves or family that concerns an adult, this may need to be shared with Mrs Appleton.
  • We know that if we share something that cannot be discussed during the session, there will always be another time to talk about it with an adult in school

Class reflection times are used at least twice a week as a way of reflecting on and discussing moral, social, spiritual and cultural issues, giving children time to share, respect, question and develop their own and others beliefs.

Planning, marking and feedback

Jigsaw comprehensive resources are used to aid planning and teaching. Marking and feedback is given in accordance to each lesson.

Assessment, recording and reporting

Each puzzle has a set of 3 attainment descriptors for each year group. These include working towards, working at and working beyond. Teachers use the best fit approach and highlight the appropriate descriptor box. A variety of strategies are used to assess pupils' progress to ensure accurate judgements are made. Work is discussed in staff meetings and planning time to ensure judgements are consistent across the school.

Year 2 use individual class journals and floor books..

Year 1 use their class floor books where evidence is shown using photographs, class lists and quotes.

Reception show photographic evidence on Tapestry, School Jotter and in class floorbooks. Tapestry is used to show progress of PSED.

Individual achievements are acknowledged in various ways including sticker reward systems, the Head teacher’s Award, Certificates/Celebration Assembly.

Contributions to school life and other aspects are reported to parents during consultations and in the teachers’ comments in the pupils’ annual reports.

Inclusion - equal opportunities, racial awareness, gender, gifted and talented, SEN

PSHE and citizenship, take account of the principles of inclusion and equal opportunities. PSHE and citizenship are overly concerned with the personal and social development of each child, and so by their very nature take as their starting point and build on the knowledge, understanding, ideas, views, perceptions, insights, interests, experiences, background, culture, concerns, values, attitudes of each child.

PSHE and citizenship go further by requiring children to explore directly the issues of inclusion and equality of opportunity. This entails appreciating similarities and valuing differences between people and cultures. It also means addressing issues such as bullying, racism and sexism.

Resource use, monitoring and acquisition

Resources that we use to support our teaching include: -

  • Jigsaw Scheme (Complete Set Foundation- Year 2)
  • Early Years Foundation Stage Development Matters- PSED.
  • Books which are available in each year group.
  • Zones of Regulation in each classroom.

Links with other subjects

It is acknowledged that PSHE & Citizenship is closely linked with other subjects and curriculum areas.

Examples of links include: -

  • P4C: developing questioning and answering skills, thinking abstractly.
  • Children’s Health Project
  • RE: Core Values, understanding about other cultures and morals, creating, developing, respecting and defending your own beliefs.
  • English: skills in enquiry and communication; stories that illustrate aspects of personal and social development.
  • British Values: becoming more aware of positive role models.
  • School Councils: respecting and valuing opinions.
  • Maths: aspects of use of money and finance; counting and sharing.
  • Science: drugs and medicines; reproduction (life cycles e.g. frogs, growing up); personal health and safety and the environment.
  • Design and Technology: health and safety; healthy eating; thinking about people's needs when designing something; using technology.
  • Computing: communicating with others, using e-mail, finding information.
  • History: using sources; historical events - what happened and why; people, events and experiences from the past.
  • Geography: looking after the environment; land use; looking at the locality and places in other parts of the world. Becoming aware of the world around us.
  • Art and Design: thinking about and responding to ideas and experiences communicated through works of art, craft and design from different times and cultures.
  • Music: making the most of their talents in playing or singing; experience of different cultures, their value and expression.
  • PE: health and safety; development of personal and social skills through group and individual activities.
  • Healthy Schools initiative: Children learn to make healthy life choices and look after their wellbeing.
  • Lunchtime clubs are offered to KS1 children throughout the year and reception in the Summer Term. This allows the children to experience enrichment opportunities with a different range of staff and pupils.
  • After School Clubs, for example Kids with Bricks. This is an opportunity for children to be part of a community other than their own class.

The promotion of the 6 key skills – Communication; Application of number; Information Technology; Working with others; Improving own learning and performance; Problem solving.

As stated by The 2014 Primary National Curriculum in England on page 5,

‘All schools should make provision for personal, social, health and economic education, drawing on good practice. Schools are also free to include other subjects or topics of their choice in planning and designing their own programme of education’.

PSHE brings a range of opportunities for the teaching and learning of key skills in communication, working with others, improving own learning and problem solving.

For the EYFS guidance can be found in the Statutory Framework for the early years foundation stage.

Thinking skills

The skills of Information processing, reasoning, enquiry, creative thinking, and evaluation are developed through our teaching and learning. Children’s’ thinking skills are challenged further through Blooms Taxonomy and P4C.

Monitoring and evaluation of teaching and learning

This policy will be monitored and evaluated regularly by the subject leaders through planning, observation and work sampling when appropriate and revised when necessary. As a school we monitor, review and where necessary revise our PSHE coverage to meet the needs of our pupils.

Staff and school development

Staff and school development needs are identified through School Development Plan and Performance Management procedures.

Review

This policy will be reviewed as part of the subject development programme and also through The Healthy Schools initiative. It is renewed annually taking account of the school’s development plan and new local and national curriculum requirements.
