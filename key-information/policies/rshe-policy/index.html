RSE September 2025

Aims and priorities for subject teaching and learning

Definition of Relationships and Sex Education

It is lifelong learning about physical, moral and emotional development in caring, loving and stable relationships.

“The focus in primary school should be on teaching the fundamental building blocks and characteristics of positive relationships, with particular reference to friendships, family relationships, and relationships with other children and with adults.” (DfE Relationships

Education, Relationships and Sex Education and Health Education (RSHE), updated July 2025)

Curriculum content for relationships education:

  • Families and people who care for me
  • Caring friendships
  • Respectful, kind relationships

Relationships and Sex is covered in these three main elements that we aim to teach:

  • Attitude and values
  • learning the importance of values including family life and loving and stable relationships.
  • learning the value of respect, love and care.
  • exploring, considering and understanding moral dilemmas.
  • Personal and social skills
  • learning to manage emotions and relationships.
  • developing self-respect and empathy for others.
  • learning to make choices based on an understanding of difference.
  • learning to recognise and deal with conflict within relationships.
  • to help children to acquire the important life skills such as decision making, communication and assertiveness.
  • Knowledge and Understanding
  • learning and understanding about growing and changing.
  • to encourage the correct use of vocabulary.
  • generate an atmosphere where children feel safe to ask questions and raise concerns.

Legal Requirements

PSHE

It is compulsory to teach Health Education and Relationships Education aspects of PSHE in primary schools (from September 2020).

KS1 National Curriculum Science

Year 1 - Identify, name, draw and label the basic parts of the human body and say which part of the body is associated with each sense.

Year 2 - Notice that animals, including humans, have offspring which grow into adults.

Sources used for the scheme of work

Early Years

In our PSHE and Jigsaw sessions, the children develop skills to form friendships and think about relationships with others. Children learn about themselves, growing up and how they grow from babies to adults.

KS1

Whilst the core RSE content is delivered through the ‘Changing Me’ Puzzle, essential work is done in previous puzzles to build children’s self-esteem, to enhance their own sense of self in regard to their body image, how to develop caring and healthy relationships and how to develop assertiveness skills in keeping themselves valued, safe and respected. This work underpins the explicit RSE content and is part of the holistic approach to RSE teaching and learning. Safeguarding

Teachers are aware that sometimes disclosures may be made during these sessions, in which case, safeguarding procedures must be followed immediately. Piece 4 of ‘Changing Me’ Puzzle will be taught by the class teacher with other members of staff present.

Use of Visitors

Visitors should complement but never substitute or replace planned provision. It is the PSHE Co-ordinators’ and teacher’s responsibility to plan the curriculum and lessons.

Relationships and Sex Guidance DfE 0116/2000

Learning Environment

All classes devise ‘ground rules’ which are drawn up with the class at the beginning of the year and are reinforced during every piece that is taught throughout the year. This is known as the class ‘Jigsaw Charter’ which is displayed in every class. Whilst the charters are tailored to individual classes, they also include the following:

  • We take turns to speak.
  • We use kind and positive words.
  • We listen to each other.
  • We have the right to pass.
  • We only use names when giving compliments or when being positive.
  • We respect our different views and opinions.
  • We respect each other’s privacy (confidentiality)
  • We know that if we say something about ourselves or family that concerns an adult, this may need to be shared with the Designated Safeguarding Lead.
  • We know that if we share something that cannot be discussed during the session, there will always be another time to talk about it with an adult in school.

All classrooms have an area for pupils to ask questions and share their concerns. In Early Years this is through a ‘Worry Monster’ and in KS1 it is through an ‘Ask it’ basket.

Resources

Jigsaw resources are predominately used to teach RSE in accordance with the needs, abilities and development stage of individuals. Class teachers will use additional suitable resources to support the Jigsaw learning intentions.

Links with other subjects

Relationship and Sex Education is taught in the context of a broad and balanced curriculum that is part of the wider framework of Personal, Social and Health Education (PSHE) and Science.

Parents right to withdraw

Parents do not have the right to withdraw their child from relationships education or the science curriculum. Parents have the right to withdraw their child from the non-statutory components of sex education within RSE. Parents wishing to withdraw their child are invited to speak to the class teacher. The class teacher will explore any concerns of the parent and look at the content and resources used for these lessons. If a parent still wishes to withdraw their child, requests for withdrawals should be made in writing to the head teacher.

Monitoring and evaluation of teaching and learning

Teachers will critically reflect on their work in delivering RSE through year group discussion and staff meetings. Pupils will have opportunities to review and reflect on their learning during RSE lessons. Pupil voice will be influential in adapting and amending planned learning. This policy will be monitored and evaluated regularly by the PSHE subject leaders through planning, observation and work sampling when appropriate and revised when necessary.

Staff and school development

Staff and school development needs are identified through School Development Plan and Performance Management procedures.

Review

This policy will be reviewed as part of the subject development programme and also through The Healthy Schools initiative. It is renewed annually taking account of the school’s development plan and new local and national curriculum requirements.
